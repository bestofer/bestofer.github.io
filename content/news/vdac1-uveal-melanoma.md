Over in Yichang, China, Rongrong Wei and Liyuan Gao went hunting through data on **uveal melanoma** (the most common eye cancer in adults) for genes tied to "R-loops" — those messy little DNA–RNA tangles that can wreck a genome. VDAC1, of all things, popped out near the top.

When they switched it off in tumour cells, the cells slowed down, stopped migrating, and started dying — but their DNA also got more tangled and damaged. So here VDAC1 isn't just the metabolism-and-death channel we usually talk about; it seems to help tumour cells keep their genome tidy enough to keep growing. New angle on an old protein, and a reminder that VDAC1 wears a lot of hats.
